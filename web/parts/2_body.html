Why this is a Sentient Futures project

Sentient Futures works on the welfare of all sentient beings as AI reshapes the world. Most AI governance is about chips, models and training runs. This project follows the physical side of AI: the buildings that run it, the heat they reject, and the water that carries that heat to animals.

Fishes, octopuses and crabs are recognised as sentient in UK law and fall within the "realistic possibility" of consciousness set out in the 2024 New York Declaration. Yet the laws now being written for data centres count megawatts, litres and jobs, not animals. Aquatic Guardian gives governments a ready-made way to count them.

Who lives there, and why they count

The law usually protects populations. But animals feel harm as individuals, and the evidence that many of these animals can feel is now strong. So the framework asks operators to count them.

Questions people ask

Is anyone actually doing this?

Yes. HiCloud's 24 MW underwater data centre off Shanghai began commercial operation in May 2026, following its Hainan facility. Microsoft ran an underwater trial in Scotland from 2018 to 2020. Coastal data centres that cool with seawater are more common still.

Isn't reusing waste heat a good thing?

Only if it happens before the water goes back to sea, and only if the reuse itself doesn't cause harm. Warm water released into a bay is discharge, not reuse. Heat sent to fish farms can mean more animals kept in poor conditions, so the framework asks for a welfare standard first.

Why count individual animals?

Because that is how harm is felt. US power plants already count the organisms their intakes kill. The framework asks data centres to do the same, and to report fishes, octopuses and crabs separately from eggs and larvae.

Is this against AI?

No. It asks that AI's physical footprint be measured and managed like any other industry's. Data centres can move computing between sites in minutes, which makes protecting water easier for them than it ever was for power plants.

The framework in five layers

Each layer produces the evidence the next one needs, and each names the instrument it plugs into.

Disclosure

A published, machine-readable record: heat to water, temperature rise, intake flow, animals by taxon and life stage, chemicals, noise, cables.

S.4214/H.R.9442 DOE reporting, EU 2024/1364, BC Hydro applications

Assessment

Thermal study at critical conditions, intake assessment, receptors including sentient taxa, cumulative climate effects.

§316(a), Fisheries Act, EU EIA and Habitats, CADA zones

Permit conditions

Closed loop by default, a numeric increment at the mixing-zone edge, 0.15 m/s screens, cold-shock ramp-down, chemical limits.

NPDES, BC EMA, member-state water permits

Monitoring

Continuous temperature, summer oxygen, intake sampling, detection-corrected surveys, published within 90 days.

Permit monitoring terms

Adaptive management

Exceedances or declines trigger a reopener or load reduction. Data centres can shift computing in minutes.

Thermal-Tidal

What to look for. Both panels use identical physics. The upwind panel smears the plume wider and understates the peak at the mixing-zone ring, because the scheme adds about 3 m²/s of mixing of its own at this grid, more than the ocean supplies at these scales. The notebook measured about 2.1 m²/s of it at 25 m. Watch the ring at slack water, when heat pools; that is when compliance is judged. Buoyant warm water stays near the surface, so the surface-layer depth sets how strong the far-field rise is.

Two numbers, two places: why the near- and far-field rises are both right

live values from the two simulations above

Why they differ. The near-field number is the centreline peak where the plume boils up above the diffuser: the most concentrated warm water anywhere, and the one screening uses because it is conservative. The far-field number is an average over a 5 m surface layer and a 50 m grid cell, 300 m downstream, after the tide has spread the heat. An average over a larger volume is always lower than the peak inside it. Regulators test compliance at a mixing-zone edge the permit defines, which usually falls between the two. That is why a full study couples a near-field model to a far-field one instead of choosing between them.

Heat reuse that counts
Reuse only reduces your footprint if the heat is captured before discharge. If it goes to a farm, the farmed animals need a named welfare standard.

      

        

        

        
  • Germany already requires new data centres to reuse at least 10% of their heat from July 2026, rising to 20% by 2028.
  • Warm water released to the sea and later "used" by a nearby farm is not reuse. The heat has already been discharged.
  • Report species, stocking density, mortality and stunning method for any animals raised on your heat.

      

    

  







  

    

      

        
About
Built in the open, for all sentient beings.
Aquatic Guardian is the second phase of a Sentient Futures fellowship project on governing ocean-cooled and underwater computing.

        

          
          
        

      

    

  

  

    

      
The project

      
Phase 1, Governing the Ocean Server Farm (Elchami, Kaplan and Daher, April 2026), mapped the governance gap for offshore compute across four stressors: heat, electromagnetic fields, noise and chemicals.

      
Phase 2 turns that analysis into things governments can adopt: a framework, model language for live instruments, a disclosure standard, and research tools for thermal transport, aquatic monitoring and contained aquaculture — applied live on the ocean cameras. Supervision: Ibrahim Elchami, Ph.D.

      
Sentient Futures works on field building for the welfare of all sentient beings as AI transforms the world. This project sits where AI infrastructure meets animal welfare.
