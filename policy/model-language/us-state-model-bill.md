# US states: model Data Centre Water and Heat Disclosure Act

For California (a reintroduced AB 93-type bill), Washington, Oregon and other coastal states. California's AB 93 (water-use disclosure) was vetoed on 11 October 2025; SB 57 (data-centre energy) was signed the same day. A water-and-heat disclosure bill that reuses existing agency reporting systems addresses the cost objections that typically accompany vetoes of this kind.

## Model bill

<!-- clause: state-bill -->
SECTION 1. Definitions. (a) "Covered data center" means a facility with installed information technology capacity of 500 kilowatts or more located in this state or in state waters. (b) "Aquatic disclosure record" means the record defined by the Aquatic Disclosure Schema, version 0.1 or a later version adopted by the [state water agency] by regulation.

SECTION 2. Reporting. The operator of each covered data center shall submit an aquatic disclosure record to the [state water agency] annually, and quarterly for any facility that withdraws surface water or seawater for cooling or discharges heated water to surface waters.

SECTION 3. Use of existing systems. The [state water agency] shall accept records through its existing electronic reporting system for NPDES permittees and shall not require duplicate submission of information already reported under a permit.

SECTION 4. Publication. The [state water agency] shall publish facility-level records within 90 days of receipt.

SECTION 5. Once-through cooling. The [state water agency] shall, within one year, determine whether its policy on once-through cooling at coastal power plants should apply to covered data centers that use once-through cooling, and shall publish that determination with its reasons.
<!-- /clause -->

## Notes for drafters

- In California, section 5 points at the State Water Resources Control Board's 2010 Once-Through Cooling Policy. The Ocean Plan desalination amendment (2015) is the closest existing model for intake and mixing-zone conditions on a new seawater user.
- In Washington, mixing-zone approvals under WAC 173-201A-400 already apply; the disclosure record adds sentience-tier biology and heat-reuse reporting.
- Oregon uses the EPA Region 10 temperature guidance (EPA 910-B-03-002) for salmonid criteria.
