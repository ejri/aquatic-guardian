# Model permit conditions

For NPDES permits (US), waste discharge authorisations under BC's Environmental Management Act, Fisheries Act authorisations (Canada), and EU member-state water permits. Bracketed values are set by the permitting authority from its own rules; the defaults shown are the US reference values.

### Temperature

<!-- clause: permit-temperature -->
1. Temperature. The increase in temperature above ambient at the edge of the mixing zone defined in Attachment [X] shall not exceed [the increment under the applicable water-quality standard, for example 12/(T-2) degrees C under WAC 173-201A-210 or 4 degrees F under the California Thermal Plan], evaluated at the critical design condition of [slack water / 10th-percentile current], [August] maximum ambient temperature and maximum discharge flow.
<!-- /clause -->

### Intake

<!-- clause: permit-intake -->
2. Intake. The maximum through-screen design intake velocity shall not exceed [0.5 ft/s (0.15 m/s)]. The permittee shall sample impingement and entrainment according to [protocol] and report organisms by taxon and life stage, identifying fishes, cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans separately and reporting eggs and larvae as distinct life stages.
<!-- /clause -->

### Shutdown and cold shock

<!-- clause: permit-cold-shock -->
3. Shutdown. Except in an emergency, the permittee shall reduce the discharge temperature rise at no more than [1] degree C per [hour] during planned shutdowns between [1 November and 31 March] and shall report any mortality observed within [500] m of the outfall within 24 hours.
<!-- /clause -->

### Chemicals

<!-- clause: permit-chemicals -->
4. Chemicals. The permittee shall report the mass of active ingredient of each biocide, antifouling agent, corrosion inhibitor and coolant used, shall not use copper-based antifouling in [critical habitat or Essential Fish Habitat], and shall not use PFAS-containing coolants.
<!-- /clause -->

### Monitoring

<!-- clause: permit-monitoring -->
5. Monitoring. The permittee shall measure temperature continuously at the outfall, at [two] points on the mixing-zone edge and at a reference site; dissolved oxygen at the mixing-zone edge from [June to September]; and occupancy of [focal taxa] at impact and reference sites using a method whose analysis corrects for detection probability. Data shall be published within 90 days.
<!-- /clause -->

### Reopener and load management

<!-- clause: permit-reopener -->
6. Reopener and load management. If the temperature condition is exceeded on more than [N] days in a season, or a detection-corrected decline in occupancy of a focal taxon is observed relative to the reference site, the permittee shall within [30] days submit a plan to reduce heat discharge, which may include shifting computational load to other facilities, and this permit may be reopened.
<!-- /clause -->

### Heat reuse for aquaculture

<!-- clause: permit-aquaculture -->
7. Heat reuse for aquaculture. Heat supplied to aquaculture counts toward any energy reuse requirement only if each farmed species is kept to [named welfare standard] and stocking density, water quality, mortality and stunning method are reported annually.
<!-- /clause -->
