# United States, federal: model language

Status, 29 September 2026: S.4214 (introduced 25 March 2026, Senate Commerce) and H.R.9442 (introduced 24 June 2026, House Energy and Commerce and Foreign Affairs) are in committee with no hearings reported. Enactment in the 119th Congress is unlikely, so the same text is also offered as a stand-alone amendment for any energy or appropriations vehicle.

Clause references follow the sponsors' section-by-section summary. Conform insertion points to the bill text before tabling.

## A. Amendments to the AI Data Center Moratorium Act

### A1. Definition

<!-- clause: us-definition -->
In section 3, at the end of the definition of "artificial intelligence data center", insert: "The term includes any such facility that is sited in, on, or beneath navigable waters, the territorial sea, or the Outer Continental Shelf, or that withdraws surface water or seawater for cooling or discharges heated water to surface waters."
<!-- /clause -->

### A2. Thermal outputs defined

<!-- clause: us-thermal-outputs -->
In the transparency provision, after "wastewater discharge and thermal outputs", insert: "reported for each facility and each quarter as: (A) heat rejected to surface water or seawater, in megawatt-hours; (B) discharge temperature and the increase above ambient, as mean, 95th percentile and maximum of hourly values; (C) design intake flow and actual intake volume, the fraction used for cooling, and the maximum through-screen intake velocity; (D) the dimensions of the area in which the applicable temperature criterion is exceeded, and the model and design conditions used to estimate it; (E) organisms impinged and entrained, by taxon and life stage, identifying fishes, cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans separately; (F) the mass of active ingredient of each biocide, antifouling agent, corrosion inhibitor and coolant released; and (G) the fraction of rejected heat recovered for beneficial use before discharge, calculated as the energy reuse factor under ISO/IEC 30134-6."
<!-- /clause -->

### A3. Environmental harm defined

<!-- clause: us-harm -->
In the conditions for lifting the moratorium, in the condition concerning harm to the environment, insert after "environment": "including water quality, aquatic and marine habitat, essential fish habitat, species listed under the Endangered Species Act, marine mammals, and the welfare of sentient aquatic animals, including fishes, cephalopod molluscs and decapod crustaceans".
<!-- /clause -->

### A4. Tribal consultation

<!-- clause: us-tribal -->
In the community-approval condition, insert: "and, where a facility or its intake or discharge may affect waters in which a Tribal Nation holds treaty-reserved or other federally recognised rights, government-to-government consultation with that Tribal Nation, with its free, prior and informed consent sought before approval".
<!-- /clause -->

## B. Stand-alone text: Data Center Aquatic Transparency Act

<!-- clause: us-standalone -->
SEC. 1. SHORT TITLE. This Act may be cited as the "Data Center Aquatic Transparency Act".

SEC. 2. DEFINITIONS. In this Act: (1) "covered data center" means a data center with installed information technology capacity of 500 kilowatts or more that withdraws surface water or seawater, discharges to surface waters, or is sited in, on or beneath navigable waters, the territorial sea or the Outer Continental Shelf; (2) "Secretary" means the Secretary of Energy, acting in consultation with the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency and the Administrator of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration.

SEC. 3. REPORTING. Not later than 1 year after enactment, the Secretary shall require the operator of each covered data center to report quarterly the information described in subparagraphs (A) through (G) of [section A2], using a machine-readable format published by the Secretary.

SEC. 4. PUBLICATION. The Secretary shall publish reports under section 3 at facility level within 90 days of receipt, except information the Secretary determines to be classified.

SEC. 5. RELATIONSHIP TO OTHER LAW. Nothing in this Act limits the application of the Federal Water Pollution Control Act, including sections 316(a) and 316(b), the Endangered Species Act of 1973, the Marine Mammal Protection Act of 1972, the Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management Act, the Coastal Zone Management Act of 1972, or the National Marine Sanctuaries Act, to a covered data center.
<!-- /clause -->

## C. Executive Order 14318: what still applies

EO 14318 (23 July 2025) directs NEPA categorical exclusions for qualifying data-centre projects. Categorical exclusions change NEPA process only. The following substantive duties are not waived and apply wherever their triggers are met:

- CWA §316(a) thermal limits and §316(b) intake rules, through the NPDES permit;
- ESA §7 consultation for federal actions that may affect listed species or critical habitat;
- MMPA incidental take authorisation;
- Magnuson-Stevens Essential Fish Habitat consultation with NMFS;
- CZMA §307 federal consistency with approved state coastal programmes;
- National Marine Sanctuaries Act §304(d) consultation and sanctuary permits;
- Rivers and Harbors Act §10 permits for structures in navigable waters;
- OCSLA leasing and permitting for facilities on the Outer Continental Shelf.

Agencies applying a categorical exclusion to a coastal or submerged data centre should document that each of these has been addressed.
